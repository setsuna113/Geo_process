The critical post-DML workflow ensures valid causal inference
After completing DML estimation, researchers face three essential tasks that determine whether their results provide reliable causal insights or misleading conclusions. The post-estimation workflow begins with comprehensive diagnostics to validate core assumptions, proceeds through rigorous hypothesis testing to establish statistical significance, and culminates in careful interpretation that translates technical results into substantive findings.
The Neyman orthogonality condition forms the foundation of all post-DML analysis. This mathematical property ensures that estimation errors in nuisance parameters don't contaminate the causal effect estimates. Modern DML implementations provide extensive diagnostic capabilities that extend far beyond traditional econometric checks. Cross-fitting diagnostics examine whether the sample-splitting procedure effectively removes overfitting bias. The key diagnostic involves examining coefficient variation across folds through the _all_coef attribute in DoubleML. Practitioners implement stability tests by comparing single versus multiple repetition estimates: stability_test = |θ̂_multiple - θ̂_single|/SE_single, where values exceeding 2 indicate concerning instability.

Overlap and common support diagnostics prevent extrapolation beyond data support. DML implementations calculate propensity scores and flag observations with extreme values (below 0.1 or above 0.9) that may unduly influence estimates. Effective sample size calculations accounting for propensity score weights reveal whether sufficient overlap exists for reliable inference. When overlap violations occur, researchers must either trim extreme observations or acknowledge limitations in their causal conclusions.
